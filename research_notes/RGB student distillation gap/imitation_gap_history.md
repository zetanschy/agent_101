# Imitation gap (privileged teacher vs. partially observing student) and the role of observation/action history in the student

Context for the report writer: the user's student (single RGB frame from 2 low-res cameras + 6 joint positions; no velocity, last action, history, or contact) imitates an 88% PPO teacher with MSE BC and reaches 27%. Its failures are at approach and grasp. The teacher's privileged inputs are joint velocity, last action (with 1–3 frames of servo dead time plus a slew limit), finger-cap contact, a cap orientation flag, and object poses. All numbers below come from the cited papers. Mappings to the user's setup are labelled as inferences.

## Q1. Theory and evidence of the imitation gap: when a privileged teacher's actions cannot be imitated, and what failures result

### Takeaway
When the teacher bases a decision on information the student cannot infer from what it sees, even perfect imitation gives the student the teacher's action averaged over every state that looks the same to it ("imitation gap"). With cross-entropy this shows up as near-uniform, hesitant behavior; with MSE it is the conditional mean. The gap grows with how privileged the teacher is. In published benchmarks, plain BC and DAgger students often reach only 5–40% while teachers reach about 100%. Several papers fix this by changing the teacher, others by mixing RL into the student's training.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition (ADVISOR, Weihs et al., NeurIPS 2021).** The student is an "f-restricted" policy: it must act the same in any two states that give the same observation. With π^IL as the best f-restricted imitator of the teacher, "We call the phenomenon that π^IL ≠ π^teach the imitation gap." Intuition from the paper: "if the information that a teacher uses to make a decision is unavailable to the student then the student has little hope of being able to mimic the teacher's decisions." — [Weihs et al. 2021, arXiv 2007.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- **1D-Lighthouse example (ADVISOR).** The goal sits at −N or +N, and the student can see only i cells. The privileged teacher always heads straight to the goal. The optimal student would commit to one side, look, and turn back if needed. Yet "the cross-entropy loss causes π^IL to be uniform in a large number of states": the imitator dithers instead of committing. — [Weihs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- **Gap size predicts performance (ADVISOR, 2D-Lighthouse).** BC students with view radius i were trained from teachers with radius j, 25 runs per pair. With i = j the student was near optimal, but "even small increases in j dramatically decrease performance" (Fig. 6). In the PoisonedDoors and LC-Switch-Once tasks, "all non-ADVISOR methods completely fail" (the baselines include BC, DAgger, BC→PPO, DAgger→PPO, and BC+PPO). The authors present the gap as one explanation for why imitating "more intelligent" teachers can produce worse policies. — [Weihs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- **ADVISOR mechanism.** The loss is L = E[w(s)·CE(π_teach, π) + (1−w(s))·L_RL]. The weight is w(s) = exp(−α·d(π_teach(s), π_aux(s))), where π_aux is an auxiliary head trained by pure imitation that estimates π^IL, and d is the KL divergence. Where the auxiliary imitator cannot match the teacher, the student switches to RL ("insubordination"). The authors note that most experiments were navigation, and that further work is "needed to validate that ADVISOR can be successful in ... interactive robotic tasks." — [Weihs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- **Impossibly Good Experts (Walsman et al., ICLR 2023).** Experts with more information than the learner can earn reward no learner can reproduce. The paper gives *necessary* criteria on the expert for recovering the best reduced-information policy from the expert's advice alone. When those criteria fail, it proposes ELF Distillation (Explorer Learning from Follower), which also uses environment reward. It beats strong baselines on MiniGrid and ViZDoom (the abstract gives no numbers). — [mlanthology abstract](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/walsman2023iclr-impossibly); [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11786)
- **A2D (Warrington et al., ICML 2021).** Core flaw: "the expert does not know what the trainee cannot see," so it may encourage actions that are suboptimal or even unsafe under partial information. A2D trains the expert to maximize the reward of the *imitating agent* (Adaptive Asymmetric DAgger), training expert and agent jointly. — [arXiv 2012.15566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15566)
- **COSIL (Nguyen et al., CoRL 2022).** A SAC-based "cross-observability soft imitation learning" method. It uses a fully observable "state expert" only during training and balances imitating it against maximizing return. It outperformed pure IL, pure RL, sequential and parallel IL+RL, and a prior state-of-the-art method on six robotics domains, and transferred to a real robot doing manipulation from pixels. — [arXiv 2211.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01991v2); [project page](https://sites.google.com/view/cosil-corl22)
- **TGRL (Shenfeld et al., ICML 2023).** Trains an auxiliary reward-only policy. If the main (teacher-guided) policy beats it, teacher-supervision weight goes up; otherwise it goes down. The hardest task is in-hand reorientation from touch only. A key ablation finding is that a joint replay buffer for the main and auxiliary policies matters. — [arXiv 2307.03186](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03186v1)
- **Scaffolder / "Privileged Sensing Scaffolds RL" (Hu et al., ICLR 2024).** Describes the imitation gap as "the student cannot recover the teacher's actions given impoverished inputs." A teacher-imitation baseline (DreamerV3+BC) fails "due to the large differences in optimal behavior between a privileged teacher and a blind student—the privileged teacher can directly pick up the object with vision, while the student policy needs to learn information gathering behavior." The BC assumption ("optimal student and teacher have the same action distributions") is violated in Wrist-Camera Pick-and-Place, Occluded Pick-and-Place, and Blind Pick. RMA and Informed Dreamer fail when "target observations o− are not predictive of the privileged observations" (e.g., "proprioception is not usually predictive of object states"). DreamerV3 without any privileged information was "a surprisingly strong performer," beating asymmetric actor-critic on some tasks. — [Hu et al. 2024, arXiv 2405.14853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14853)
- **Scaffolder ablation.** Each scaffolded component was replaced by an unprivileged one, and every ablation dropped performance. The most important component differed by task: exploration for Blind Pick, the critic for Blind Cube Rotation, the world model for Blind Locomotion, and the representation (reconstructing privileged object pose and touch from images) for RGB Cube Rotation. — [Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14853)
- **Student-Informed Teacher Training (Messikommer et al., ICLR 2025).** Quadrotor obstacle avoidance from vision, success rate: BC 0.05, DAgger 0.08, HLRL 0.31, DWBC 0.35, COSIL 0.30, their method without alignment 0.38, with alignment 0.46. Teachers reach over 98%. Drawer opening (manipulation), success rate: BC 0.16±0.15, DAgger 0.34±0.31, HLRL 0.61, DWBC 0.63, COSIL 0.56, ours without alignment 0.61, ours with alignment 0.88±0.07. All teachers reach 100%. (The paper's text is internally inconsistent on some manipulation numbers, e.g., it says "0.77 vs 0.47 DAgger"; the table numbers are given here.) — [arXiv 2412.09149v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09149v2); [project page](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/sitt/)
- **Guided Policy Optimization (Li, Xie, Lu; ICLR 2026).** States the "impossibly good teacher" problem as the student only learning "the average of the teacher's actions for each observation" (TigerDoor example). It co-trains a privileged guider that is kept close to the learner (GPO-Clip: a PPO-style double clip). It reports strong results on partially observable or noisy continuous control and on memory tasks. Only the abstract-level claims were checked. — [arXiv 2505.15418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15418v2); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009401)
- **RAIL (Mammadov, Zuidberg Dos Martires, Stork; arXiv 9 Nov 2026).** "When the teacher relies on privileged information, identical student observations can correspond to contradictory teacher actions." Teacher success / student success: Corners BC 1.00/0.24, DAgger 1.00/0.38, RAIL 1.00/1.00. TunnelVision BC 0.12, DAgger 0.15, RAIL 1.00. CollectHealth (RGB student) BC 0.28, DAgger 0.84, RAIL 0.98. QuarterCheetah return: BC −268, DAgger −484, RAIL 2510. No manipulation tasks. — [arXiv 2611.12251](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12251)
- **Distilling Realizable Students from Unrealizable Teachers (Kim, Chin, Vasudev, Choudhury; arXiv May 2025).** With state aliasing, many teacher states map to one student observation, so the same input receives conflicting labels. Their claim: "DAgger fails entirely due to accumulating conflicting labels from the aliased teacher states." Methods: CritiQ (query the teacher only at critical states found by a discriminator) and ReTRy (reset RL from teacher-visited states). Tasks are MuJoCo drawer, block, and navigation search, plus a real Hello Stretch. ReTRy reached 100% on the real robot; CritiQ dropped from 78% in simulation to 66% on the real robot. — [arXiv 2505.09546](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09546)

### Inferences
- In the user's task, **finger-cap contact** and **joint velocity / last action** (what is still "in the pipe" through the 1–3 frame dead time and slew limit) are the teacher inputs least inferable from one 96×72 + 64×48 frame plus joint positions. The binary open-side-up flag may or may not be visible at that resolution. Grasp-closure timing and approach-speed decisions that key on these inputs are exactly the "aliased" labels these papers describe. An MSE student gets their conditional mean: e.g., partly closing while still approaching, or creeping. That fits the observed failures (cap pushed away, mug knocked, no pickup). The fit is suggestive, not proven.
- DAgger alone does not close an information gap (RAIL TunnelVision 0.15, SITT quadrotor 0.08, Kim et al. "fails entirely"). It helps in RAIL's RGB CollectHealth (0.84), which suggests DAgger mostly fixes covariate shift and copycat (Q3), not missing information.
- Benchmark evidence for "student-aware" teachers is strongest in the manipulation setting closest to the user's: SITT drawer opening went 0.34 (DAgger) to 0.88. The no-alignment variant reached 0.61, so the teacher-side penalty alone already helps a lot.

### Gaps
- No quantitative numbers for A2D, TGRL, COSIL (outside SITT's table), or Impossibly Good Experts were extracted; only abstracts were read. The exact "necessary criteria" in Walsman et al. were not retrieved (OpenReview was gated).
- Scaffolder's per-task numeric scores were not extracted (figures only).
- No paper found that studies a contact-sensor-equipped teacher distilled into an RGB-only grasping student specifically.

## Q2. Practical remedies: student-aware teacher, memory in the student, auxiliary prediction of privileged state, proprioceptive velocity

### Takeaway
Remedies fall into four groups:
1. Make the teacher imitable: penalize teacher–student divergence, train the teacher on student-recoverable representations, or co-train with constraints (SITT, A2D, GPO, RAIL).
2. Give the student memory so hidden variables become inferable from history (TCN/GRU; Lee et al. 2020: TCN-100 better than TCN-1, GRU in between).
3. Supervise the student's latent toward the teacher's privileged latent (RMA, Lee et al.), which only works when the privileged signal is predictable from student inputs (Scaffolder's RMA failure case).
4. Mix in RL where imitation is impossible (ADVISOR, TGRL, COSIL).

Fighting Copycat directly tested a student given positions only (no velocity), close to the user's case.

### Cited Findings
- **Student-aware teacher via KL penalty (SITT).** Teacher reward r′ = r − λ₁·KL(π_T ‖ π̂_S). π̂_S is a *proxy student* that sees teacher observations, so no images need to be rendered. The loss also gets a λ₂·KL term (λ₂ = 0.01 for manipulation), plus an L1 alignment of student encoder features to teacher features. Manipulation ablation (success): λ₁ = 0.1 gave 0.81, 0.075 gave 0.77, 0.05 (default) 0.88, 0.025 0.95. No penalty: 0.74 ("a 14% drop"). Penalty without KL-gradient: 0.47. Without the shared decoder: 0.62. — [arXiv 2412.09149v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09149v2)
- **Teacher trained only on student-recoverable information (RAIL).** InfoNCE aligns teacher and student encoders into a shared latent space. The teacher's RL runs on that space, with gradient blocking so RL does not shape the encoders. This took the gap Δ to 0.00–0.01 in three tasks (table in Q1). — [arXiv 2611.12251](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12251)
- **Expert adapted to the agent (A2D):** the expert is trained to maximize the imitating agent's reward. — [arXiv 2012.15566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15566)
- **Privileged Information Dropout (Kamienny et al., 2020):** feeds privileged information as an input with dropout instead of distilling. On a simple partially observed environment it beat distillation and auxiliary-task alternatives (abstract-level only). — [arXiv 2005.09220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09220)
- **Memory in the student (Lee et al., Science Robotics 2020).** The proprioceptive student takes a history of 60-D measurements (base and joint states, gravity vector, FTG phases, previous foot-position targets) at 0.02 s per step, with N up to 100 (2 s). It uses a TCN with 3 dilated causal conv layers. History length mattered little on slopes but a lot on steps; under a 50 N lateral push, TCN-100 deviated 35.5% less than TCN-1. A GRU student fell between TCN-20 and TCN-100, and the TCN trained much faster. A TCN-100 student without the latent-matching loss had lower step success. A TCN-20 trained directly with RL (no teacher) failed. — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.11251)
- **RMA (Kumar et al., RSS 2021).** The adaptation module estimates the teacher's privileged "extrinsics" latent from recent *states and actions* with a 1-D CNN, described as analogous to a Kalman filter. It runs at 10 Hz while the base policy runs at 100 Hz. History length is about 50 steps (Fig. 1 indexes back to x_{t−50}; the explicit "H = 50" comes from a secondary summary only). — [arXiv 2107.04034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04034)
- **Auxiliary prediction fails when the privileged signal is not inferable.** Scaffolder: RMA "excels in Noisy Monkey, where regressing the privileged true state ... is relatively easy, yet completely fails in Blind Pick." Scaffolder's own *representation* scaffold (reconstructing privileged pose and touch from images) was the most important component in RGB Cube Rotation. — [Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14853)
- **Speed-prediction auxiliary head (Codevilla et al., ICCV 2019).** Adding a speed-prediction branch to the image-based driving policy (CILRS) mitigates the causal-confusion "inertia problem" and "can substantially improve the success rate" through regularization. Failures from inertia *increase* with more training data. — [arXiv 1904.08980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08980v1) (details from search snippets; full text not read)
- **Velocity-free (positions-only) students (Fighting Copycat, Wen et al., NeurIPS 2020).** MuJoCo tasks were made partially observed "by setting the states to only include the joint positions and exclude ... velocity and external force." Single-frame BC (BC-SO) was poor. Adding one past frame (BC-OH, H = 2) helped in 5/6 environments. PO-Walker2d: BC-SO 363, BC-OH 592, theirs 1296, expert 2428. PO-HalfCheetah: −38, 820, 1250, expert 1336. PO-Hopper: 275, 293, 1086, expert 1780. — [arXiv 2010.14876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)
- **Hybrid IL+RL where imitation is impossible:** ADVISOR, TGRL, COSIL (Q1), and Scaffolder, which uses privileged sensors for the critic, world model, exploration, and representation rather than for action labels. — [Weihs 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173); [Shenfeld 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03186v1); [Nguyen 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01991v2); [Hu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14853)

### Inferences
- Cheapest options for the user, ordered by how directly the evidence supports them:
  1. Add joint velocity (finite-difference of positions) and/or a short frame/proprio history. Fighting Copycat's positions-only setup is the closest analogue: history helped 5/6 tasks.
  2. Retrain or fine-tune the teacher with the inputs the student cannot infer (contact sensors, possibly the flag) removed or noised, or with a SITT-style KL penalty toward a proxy student. Contact is the most obvious candidate, given Scaffolder's note that proprioception and vision rarely predict touch.
  3. Add an auxiliary head that predicts inferable privileged quantities (cap pose relative to EE, mug rim, orientation flag) from images. Do not expect one frame to predict contact or the in-pipe actions.
- No source directly ablated "remove the contact sensor from the teacher, then distill." The recommendation is an inference from the imitation-gap theory plus SITT and RAIL.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports a student-success ablation from removing or noising specific privileged teacher channels (e.g., contact) in manipulation distillation.
- Kamienny et al.'s numeric results were not extracted.
- No direct manipulation ablation of frame stacking vs. GRU vs. TCN in an RGB student distilled from an RL teacher was found (only locomotion, Lee et al.).

## Q3. Copycat / causal confusion: when do history and previous actions hurt a BC student, and does DAgger fix it?

### Takeaway
Giving a BC student history, and especially past actions, lets it learn a shortcut: "repeat the previous action." That shortcut fits held-out demos well but fails at action changepoints (start moving, close the gripper, change direction). Feedforward history BC still usually beats single-frame BC when observations lack velocity, but falls well short of the expert. An RNN can be worse. DAgger fixes copycat: on-policy relabeling breaks the past-action/next-action correlation, and with 1k queries it approaches expert performance. Offline fixes include adversarially removing past-action information, upweighting keyframes, residual prediction, instrumental-variable methods, and past-token prediction. For diffusion policies the opposite failure (history under-used) is reported.

### Cited Findings
- **Causal confusion (de Haan, Jayaraman, Levine, NeurIPS 2019).** BC ignores causal structure, causing "causal misidentification," where "access to more information can yield worse performance." Their fix is targeted interventions via environment interaction or expert queries, compared against DAgger. Per Wen et al., all of de Haan's experiments "explicitly add a_{t−1} into o_t to induce causal confusion." — [arXiv 1905.11979](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11979); [Wen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)
- **Copycat problem (Wen et al., NeurIPS 2020).** Many prior works report that H = 1 is optimal. With H > 1, held-out expert log-likelihood *improves* (no overfitting) while environment reward drops. The nuisance correlate is the previous expert action, which can be recovered from observation history. Evidence (Table 1): an MLP predicting a_t from a_{t−1..t−9} has lower error on BC-OH rollouts than on expert rollouts in all 6 environments, e.g., Walker2d 0.46e-2 vs. 2.47e-2 and Humanoid 0.18e-1 vs. 6.93e-1. Copycat Walker agents fall because "the right knee repeats previous actions," and copycat Half-Cheetahs "did not ever start to move." — [arXiv 2010.14876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)
- **Fighting Copycat, Table 2 (partially observed MuJoCo, positions only, return):**
  - BC-OH (RNN) was *worse* than feedforward BC-OH: Ant −311 vs. 1750; Walker 190 vs. 592.
  - Dropout-BC "performs uniformly poorly" (Ant 830).
  - Their adversarial method removes a_{t−1} information from the embedding: Ant 2150, Hopper 1086, Walker 1296.
  - DAgger with 100 queries is comparable to their offline method (Ant 2090, Hopper 978, Walker 701).
  - DAgger with 1k queries is "significantly better, approaching the performance of the RL expert": Ant 2240 vs. expert 2348, Walker 2170 vs. 2428, HalfCheetah 1270 vs. 1336. Hopper (1120 vs. 1780) and Humanoid (812 vs. 4963) remain far off.

  — [arXiv 2010.14876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876)
- **Keyframe-focused BC (Wen et al., ICML 2021):** upweights "demonstration keyframes corresponding to expert action changepoints." Reports consistent gains over all baselines on image-based MuJoCo and enables history-based imitation in CARLA (no numbers in the abstract). — [arXiv 2106.06452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06452)
- **Residual action prediction (Chuang, Yang, Wen, Gao; ECCV 2022):** history-based imitation "can underperform imitation from the latest observation" because of copying previous actions. Their architecture is built so it cannot act from previous actions. CARLA supplement: green-light compliance 30.6% for BC-OH, 42.1% for Keyframe, 66.3% for theirs. — [ECVA paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/2802_ECCV_2022_paper.php); [supplement](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136990386-supp.pdf)
- **Temporally correlated noise (Swamy, Choudhury, Bagnell, Wu; ICML 2022):** confounding noise spanning several timesteps creates spurious state–action correlations. Instrumental-variable regression with past states as instruments recovers the expert policy: DoubIL with a simulator, ResiduIL fully offline. Both "compare favorably to behavioral cloning" on simulated control. — [PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/swamy22a.html)
- **Inertia problem (Codevilla et al. 2019):** when speed is an input, a low-speed/no-acceleration spurious correlation makes agents stay stopped. This is the driving form of copycat, mitigated by a speed-prediction head. — [arXiv 1904.08980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08980v1)
- **Past-Token Prediction (Torne, Tang, Liu, Finn; 2025): naive history hurts, and the cause depends on the policy class.** Regression policies over-rely on past actions, with action predictability "even exceeding that of the expert demonstrations" (copycat). Diffusion policies *under-use* history, with predictability about 10–100× weaker than the expert. Adding past actions as input without PTP did not help. Simulated success, PTP / naive history / no history:
  - Square 0.89 / 0.17 / 0.79
  - Tool-Hang 0.75 / 0.00 / 0.51
  - Transport 0.67 / 0.00 / 0.60
  - ALOHA 0.98 / 0.20 / 0.28
  - Long Square 0.93 / 0.03 / 0.12
  - Push-T 0.62 / 0.59 / 0.67 (no history wins)

  On the real robot: no history about 15% average, naive history "near-zero success on three of four tasks," PTP about 70%. Method: an auxiliary loss predicting past action tokens a_{t−k..t} along with future ones. Encoder embeddings are cached, reaching the same performance in 20% of the training time. A test-time check picks the sample whose reconstructed past actions match the executed ones. — [arXiv 2505.09561](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09561)

### Inferences
- The user's actions are *deltas on an integrated target* and the teacher is smooth PPO, so consecutive actions are probably strongly autocorrelated. That is the regime where copycat is worst. Feeding the raw last action to an MSE student offline is risky. Safer ways to give the student "what's in the pipe": (a) feed the current *commanded joint target* (or target minus measured position) instead of the raw last delta; (b) feed history but add PTP-style past-action prediction, Wen-style adversarial removal, or keyframe upweighting around grasp-closure and contact transitions; (c) use DAgger, the strongest copycat fix in Wen et al. (1k queries came close to the expert in 4/6 environments).
- Fighting Copycat's PO-MuJoCo results suggest that with positions-only proprioception, a short frame/proprio history (H = 2) usually helps despite copycat, and a vanilla RNN can hurt. Prefer short feedforward stacks or TCNs, plus a regularizer, over a naive GRU.
- DAgger fixes copycat and covariate shift but *not* the information gap (Q1). The user likely needs both: a more imitable teacher, and DAgger or history-with-regularization.

### Gaps
- Per-environment numbers for the keyframe method (Wen 2021) and Swamy's methods were not extracted.
- No manipulation-grasping study was found that isolates copycat from a last-action input in a vision student distilled from an RL teacher.

## Q4. Action delay / actuator latency makes the state non-Markov; how teacher–student works give the student action history

### Takeaway
With action or observation delay, the current observation is not a Markov state. The standard fix (delayed MDPs) appends the last K sent actions, where K is the maximum total delay. Without them, learning can collapse: SAC at a one-step action delay is "near-random." Locomotion teacher–student work therefore gives the student proprioceptive *and* past action/target history (Lee et al. 2020; RMA), so the student can estimate hidden dynamics and in-flight commands.

### Cited Findings
- **Bouteiller et al., "Reinforcement Learning with Random Delays" (ICLR 2021).** "Any delay longer than one time-step violates the Markov assumption." The fix: augment the observation with "at least the last K sent actions," with K "the combined maximum possible observation and action delay" (the Random-Delay MDP). The authors credit Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht (2003) with "the first discussion about Delayed Markov Decision Processes"; Walsh et al. (2008) treat constant delays. Empirically, at observation delay 0 and action delay 1, naive SAC on delayed observations alone "has near-random results," while SAC with the action buffer learns. Their DCAC method (partial trajectory resampling) "clearly dominates" under random WiFi-like delays; all methods fail on uniformly random-delayed Ant. — [arXiv 2010.02966](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02966)
- **Lee et al. 2020.** The student history includes previous foot-position *targets* (past actions in task space) along with joint states, over up to 2 s. Its learned actuator network takes joint position error and velocity at t, t−0.01 s, and t−0.02 s, and the teacher's observations include these past errors and velocities, "letting the policy exploit actuator dynamics." The paper does not explicitly discuss communication latency. — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.11251)
- **RMA:** the student's adaptation module takes the recent *state–action* history to estimate privileged extrinsics. — [arXiv 2107.04034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04034)

### Inferences
- With 1–3 frames of randomized dead time plus a slew limit, the user's student needs at least the last 3 actions (or the current commanded target plus a short buffer) to be Markov in the Bouteiller sense. The teacher has its last action but not 3, so it may itself be only approximately Markov. A student with *no* action information is missing the dominant short-horizon dynamics state. That is consistent with failures in fast contact phases (pushing the cap away).
- Taken together, this conflicts with Q3's copycat risk. Resolutions: feed the commanded target (state of the integrator) rather than raw deltas; use the action buffer with past-token prediction or adversarial de-correlation; or collect data with DAgger. Note also that Lee et al. and RMA train the student with on-policy (DAgger-style) data, which Wen et al. show removes copycat.

### Gaps
- Chen et al. "Delay-aware model-based RL" and the original Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht 2003 paper were not fetched; they are cited only via Bouteiller et al.
- No numbers were found for a manipulation student with vs. without action history under actuator latency.
- RMA's exact history length (50) is unverified from the primary text.

## Q5. Multimodality: MSE averaging when the student cannot disambiguate the teacher's action, and fixes

### Takeaway
When teacher actions are a function of information the student lacks, the student's target is a *distribution* given its observation. MSE returns the mean, which can be an action no mode would take. ADVISOR's cross-entropy analysis gives the discrete version (uniform action probabilities). Expressive heads (diffusion, chunked CVAE/transformer) handle multimodality in standard BC benchmarks, but they cannot add the missing information. Recent distillation work (2025–26) emphasizes that conflicting labels at aliased observations break BC and DAgger. Direct evidence for "diffusion student distilled from an RL teacher beats an MSE student" was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Discrete analogue of averaging:** under the imitation gap, "the cross-entropy loss causes π^IL to be uniform in a large number of states." — [Weihs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- GPO describes the student as only able to learn "the average of the teacher's actions for each observation." RAIL: "identical student observations can correspond to contradictory teacher actions." Kim et al.: DAgger "accumulat[es] conflicting labels from the aliased teacher states." — [GPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15418v2); [RAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12251); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09546)
- **Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., RSS 2023):** "gracefully handling multimodal action distributions." Beats prior state-of-the-art by an average of 46.9% across 12 tasks from 4 benchmarks. Uses receding-horizon control and visual conditioning. — [arXiv 2303.04137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137)
- **ACT (Zhao et al., RSS 2023):** "learns a generative model over action sequences" (action chunking with a CVAE transformer) to counter compounding errors and non-stationary human demos. 80–90% success on 6 real fine-grained tasks from 10 minutes of demonstrations. — [arXiv 2304.13705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705)
- **Caveat on expressive heads with history:** past-token prediction finds that diffusion policies *under-use* history; naive-history diffusion fell to 0.00 on Tool-Hang and Transport. An expressive head plus history still needs an auxiliary objective. — [arXiv 2505.09561](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09561)
- A secondary search snippet says the ULTRA humanoid paper (arXiv 2603.03279) uses a latent: a privileged encoder infers a latent during training, a prior predicts it from masked student observations, and the student acts conditioned on a sampled latent. Not verified from the primary text. — [arXiv 2603.03279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03279)

### Inferences
- For the user's grasp phase, a diffusion or chunked head would represent "close now" vs. "keep approaching" as separate modes instead of their mean. That can stop the half-committed, pushing behavior. But mode *selection* would still be a coin flip if the deciding signal (contact, in-pipe motion) is invisible. Fixing information (teacher-side restriction, history, velocity) comes first; expressiveness is complementary.
- Action chunking also partly compensates for actuator dead time: open-loop chunks commit to a plan across the delay. This is an inference, not directly measured in the cited sources for latency.

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly compares an MSE student with a diffusion or mixture student when distilling a *privileged RL teacher* in manipulation, i.e., how much of the gap is multimodality vs. missing information.
- ACT's chunking ablation numbers were not extracted (abstract only).
